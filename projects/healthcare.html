The business problem

Appointment operations decide how patients actually reach care. When completion, cancellation, and no show patterns are not understood, schedules look full while access quietly fails: some specialties and locations carry the missed appointments, long lead times may push patients to drop out, and provider workload spreads unevenly. This project analyzes healthcare appointment operations to identify patterns in appointment outcomes, scheduling, patient access, wait times, patient satisfaction, and provider workload.

Why the traceability matrix matters

Analysis work often drifts away from the questions it was commissioned to answer. The traceability matrix is the control against that drift: every requirement in this case study is written down first, and every query and dashboard view exists because a requirement asked for it. A reviewer can start at any finding, follow the matrix back to the requirement, and follow the requirement back to the business problem. That chain is the difference between a set of charts and a piece of business analysis.

My approach on this project

I defined the business problem and objectives before touching data, wrote the stakeholder analysis and requirements next, and only then built the SQL analysis and reporting material. The order matters to me: the questions decide what the data work is for, and the documentation keeps the reasoning visible after the analysis is done.
